marco: via, measured

What a via costs on Relationships and BlockPairs: the typical calls, the accounts built to make it slow, an uncheckpointed mass unsubscribe, and the read cap at 1,000 against 100. In the engine, with the handlers' work and no codec, on a synthetic graph shaped like the network's lists.

Each number is the latency of the read a handler does for one request, names to ids, the direct relations, the vias and the URIs out, with every page in memory (the snapshot was loaded whole). That's the CPU a server spends on the request short of decoding it and writing the response. A via reads each subscriber's newest records up to the cap, once a call, only after the member's list count says it's on a list, and probes the member's in set with them.

The synthetic graph

marco-harness via-synth writes it: follows with a long tail, a fifth of the accounts listable, lists a tenth as many as accounts with long-tailed sizes (one of 50,000 members), three in ten of them mod lists. 5% of accounts subscribe to a few lists, an eighth of the listable to 100-400, and 2,000 listable accounts to 1,200 each, past the default cap. One account subscribes to 200,010 lists, one is on 50,000, and 0.2% are taken down.

The vias add little to a typical call, since most of the others are on no list and cost one count each. The heavy viewer costs one read of 1,000 records whatever it holds past them. The BlockPairs worst case reads 2,000 subscribers at the cap, two million records, and is the one shape where the cap sets the cost: tens of milliseconds at 1,000, a few at 100. Since reads are open, an operator whose API listener takes requests it doesn't control can lower --via-max-records to bound it.

The cap: 1,000 against 100

The same shapes on the same snapshot and the same draws with --cap 100. The cap trades a bounded read for the lists past it: at 100, a list block further back in an account's subscriptions isn't seen, and the answer says it's truncated. Two things to read the hits with. The synthetic graph gives an eighth of the listable accounts 100-400 subscriptions and 2,000 of them 1,200, to stress the reads, so it has far more heavy subscribers than the network and the hits a lower cap misses here are an upper bound. And each run's worst case draws from the accounts at or past its own cap, so the worst-case rows compare costs, not hits.

An uncheckpointed mass unsubscribe

The bench then deleted the heavy subscriber's newest 150,000 subscriptions (143.59 ms to apply) and ran three shapes before any checkpoint. A read passes at most 10 deleted or replaced records per record of the cap (10,000 at the default), then stops with what it found and counts as cut, so the account costs a bounded read until a checkpoint folds the deletes.

Method

marco-harness via-synth --out /tmp/via.marco
MARCO_MEMORY=2GiB marco-harness via-bench --snapshot /tmp/via.marco --out via-1000.json
MARCO_MEMORY=2GiB marco-harness via-bench --snapshot /tmp/via.marco --cap 100 --out via-100.json

Release build. MARCO_MEMORY holds the whole snapshot, so via-bench loads every page before it times anything (loaded: true). Each request runs on one thread, one at a time. The numbers are from an 8-vCPU Linux VM on a laptop, so read them as relative costs: a server box with faster cores and more of them is faster on each and serves many at once. marco-harness perf takes the same via entries in a mix file for load through the server.
